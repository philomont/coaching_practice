More specifically, how does a PhD scientist-turned-AI practitioner and
leader decide all of a sudden that coaching is for him? I guess that is
the funny thing about resumes: they tell a story about how useful you
are to an organization by laying out all of your contributions to
"progress," but they also completely ignore all the people in your life
who helped you become the person you are. Gratitude in a resume can
usually only be expressed as a humble-brag.

In reality, none of our successes belong solely to us. No matter how
much you pull up on those bootstraps, someone had to help you into
those boots in the first place. We use our imagination to abstract away
uncontrolled variables, but our world, the one we keep trying to
improve by solving one problem after another, is still filled with
people who are unpredictable, self-conscious, and worthy of our
effort. I have had many trusted mentors, coaches, and teachers, and I
wouldn't be who I am today without their investment in my growth. All
of my accomplishments are shared with them. Knowing that doesn't make
my achievements feel any less significant; instead, it makes them feel
even bigger because they have value beyond what I alone can place on
them.

I have also had times when I felt stuck, listless, and depressed, times
when I wanted to go back to the drawing board of life and solve my
problems alone. As an introvert, I always find this a natural defensive
strategy, one that makes me feel less exposed. But after wading around
in my own existential dread, I eventually realize that I cannot
self-validate my way out of a rut. Reluctantly, yet in my own best
interest, I come around to the fact that I need help from an outside
advisor, which is usually when I start feeling better. Seeking trusted
perspectives from friends, family, colleagues, and professionals is
what most of us need in these moments. The more perspectives you
gather, and the more varied they are, the clearer your path forward
becomes.

I feel a great sense of duty when someone comes to me for guidance.
When they place their trust in me, I have an opportunity to shape the
world around me in a small, positive way through my worldview and
experience. It allows me to honor those who helped me and pay it
forward in my own unique way. It connects me to others in a way that
abstract problem-solving never could. Don't get me wrong: I love
science and analytical thinking, but coaching fulfills my sense of
purpose in a completely different way. By being both a technologist
and a coach, I bring a unique approach to translating EQ into
measurable ROI. That is why I maintain a formal coaching practice.

Gratitude for those who have helped me, along with the opportunity to
make a positive impact on my fellow problem solvers: that is what
brings me to coaching.

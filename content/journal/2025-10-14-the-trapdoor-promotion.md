title: The trapdoor promotion
date: 2025-10-14

excerpt: Why getting everything you wanted can feel like falling, and how to turn the free-fall into steady ground.
---

No one warns you that the best day of your career can feel like
falling through a floor. The promotion you worked years for lands,
everyone congratulates you. Within a week you’re lying awake,
convinced you’ve been promoted one level past your competence.

**The trapdoor feeling is not evidence.** It’s the normal
disorientation of a bigger job: wider ambiguity, fewer
instructions, more people watching. The skills that earned the
role are rarely the ones the role now requires. Which is exactly
why the gap feels personal, even though it’s structural.

Three things reliably help.

## Name the gap precisely

Vague dread is sticky; a specific list is workable. Write down
what the new role actually demands: decision-making with
incomplete information, managing peers, holding a longer horizon.
Mark the two you feel least ready for. That list, not your
imposter voice, is the real curriculum.

## Borrow structure while you build judgment

Senior roles run on judgment, and judgment grows from experience
you don’t have yet. So borrow structure: a 90-day plan you review
with a mentor, explicit decision criteria for the calls that
terrify you, a standing weekly question with your manager about
where the real risks are. Structure isn’t a crutch. It’s scaffolding
around the gap while your judgment catches up.

## Tell one person the truth

Pick someone safe and say the quiet part out loud: *“I’m in deeper
than I’m used to.”* Almost every leader I’ve worked with describes
this exact night. Saying it once usually costs less than carrying
it for a year.

The free-fall feeling fades when the gap gets named, scaffolded,
and shared. That’s not a pep talk. It’s the mechanics of how
people grow into bigger roles: deliberately, with company, one
honest step at a time.

title: Seeing the transition before it sees you

excerpt: On recognizing the early signs of career change — before the current decides for you.
---

Career transitions rarely announce themselves. They arrive first
as small irritations: the meeting you keep dreading, the win that
lands flat, the growing pile of work that’s easy for you but
meaningless. We tend to treat these as weather. Often they’re
compass readings.

## The three early signals

**First, competence without curiosity.** When you’re great at what
you do and no longer curious about it, the work has stopped
developing you — long before the results show decay.

**Second, envy with a target.** If certain tasks energize you far
out of proportion to what they pay, or if you feel a pull toward
what a peer or a different role gets to do, your values are
already voting. Envy, examined honestly, is one of the most
useful — and least discussed — career signals.

**Third, futures you refuse to picture.** Notice which possible
futures you avoid imagining. We skip the scenarios that feel
impossible; sometimes that’s practicality, and sometimes the
impossibility is the only thing that isn’t real.

## Read them early, act gently

None of these mean quit. They mean *pay attention, and start
small experiments* — a project that pulls toward the signal, a
conversation with someone living the alternative, a course, a
short secondment. Transition handled early is a series of
low-stakes tests. Transition ignored is a single high-stakes
decision made under exhaustion.

The goal isn’t to see the future. It’s to stop pretending the
present is permanent — and to give yourself enough honest runway
to choose, rather than merely react, when the current decides to
turn.

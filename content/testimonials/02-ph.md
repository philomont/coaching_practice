I have run coach-training programs for a decade, and I can tell
you within a session whether someone will make coaching their
craft or just collect the credential. Phil did something that is
still uncommon: he built a supervision practice around it before
taking a single client, and he still checks his work when the
work is hard.

His transition through leadership roles into coaching was not a
career pivot with a plan; it was slower, more careful, and more
honest than that. He coaches the way he changed: he meets people
where they actually are, names what is true, and keeps the next
step small and real.

I now send him clients without hesitation, particularly
executives in the middle of big, high-stakes change. His
practice is built exactly the way he coaches: patiently, and
around the person in front of him.

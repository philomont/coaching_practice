For twenty years I’ve worked alongside
leaders in seasons of change — the promotions that outgrew them,
the pivots that looked reckless from the outside, the quiet
question of whether the current path was still the right one.
What I’ve learned is that the best work isn’t scripted.
It’s a close, honest conversation that unfolds over time.

Today I work one-on-one with leaders and professionals:
understanding your values, naming your goals in your own words, and
iterating with you on concrete steps that bring your leadership
objectives to life — using the resources you actually have, not
a fantasy version of them.

I came to coaching from the physical sciences, and that background
shapes how I work. I'm comfortable with evidence, models, and the
discipline of changing one variable at a time — so the practice is
pragmatic: we observe what's actually happening, form a hypothesis,
try the smallest real experiment, and study what comes back. Leaders
in technical organizations tend to recognize the kind of rigor they
already trust, pointed at decisions and people instead of systems
and data.

It also keeps the work creative. Working from observation and
iteration — rather than from a borrowed script — leaves room for
insight to show up in unexpected ways, because we're following what
you actually notice, not a pre-approved path.

Alongside leadership coaching, I've spent years on the water
coaching paddling teams — dragon boating and canoeing. Steering a
crew of very different people toward the same finish line taught me
a lot of what shows up in my sessions: shared rhythm beats
individual strength, and honest, immediate feedback lands best
when it's given in service of the goal.
